The remote-resolution playbook this RCA argues for

  1. Ask what the customer can see, not what is broken. “Is the Wi-Fi name showing in the TV's network list?” splits the 15 cases straight down the middle — SSID missing is a device or WLAN fault, SSID present is a speed, coverage or TV-side fault.
  2. SSID not visible anywhere → treat as a dead Wi-Fi device. Four of these ended in a device swap and a reset did not save any of them. Skip the reset loop, raise the replacement.
  3. SSID visible on the phone but not the TV → rename the SSID from the WLAN settings. Two cases cleared on that alone, remotely, with no visit.
  4. TV joins nothing while the phone is fine → check the band. Older TVs are 2.4 GHz only; confirm the model before booking a visit. One customer needed the TV shop, not us.
  5. Buffering, not disconnection → read the line before dispatching. Check ISP throughput and optical receive power first; −30 dBm is already out of range and no CSP visit will fix it.
  6. Camera down but Wi-Fi up → suspect the LAN run to the DVR. Where the DVR sits in a different shop, the RJ45 crimp is the first thing to test.
  7. Close the “no fault at our end” cases explicitly. Three of 15 were an uninstalled app, a repaired TV that forgets credentials, and an unsupported band. They must be dispositioned as customer-premise, or they age in the tracker as our breach.
